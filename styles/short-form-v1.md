# Style profile: short-form v1 (talking-head shorts)

Built 2026-11-03 from a 30-reel study of @nolan_killeen, @hayeskrause and @cameronprofittt
(`~/Obsidian/Luxvoni/Research/References/2026-11-03-creator-study-nolan-hayes-cameron.md`).
Structure and production only, never their identity.

**How to use:** tell `/video-script` or `/youtube-edit` "use style short-form-v1". Rules here
override `skills/youtube-edit/STYLE.md` for that video wherever they conflict. Every rule is
*(candidate)* until Andrew confirms it with his own results.

## Pick the format first (edit density follows the format)
- **SF1 Event or story** (live outreach, skit, hot take, results reveal): one continuous take
  or very few cuts (≤ 4/min). No decorative B-roll. The event is the motion.
- **SF2 Explainer or list** ("If I woke up tomorrow…", numbered system): breaths removed
  (~250 wpm), a new angle or header per step, literal inserts or screenshots on the nouns
  covering 40–80% of runtime, a "SCREENSHOT THIS" recap card mid-video.
- **SF3 Dash one-liner**: 12–16s, one take, a 1s pause after the turn word ("but…"), no CTA.
  The best format for one-variable tests.

## Hook (first 3 seconds)
- **SF4** First word spoken in the first frame (≤ 0.3s). No intro, logo or greeting.
- **SF5** Motion in the first second without a cut: a swing-in (start recording pointed
  away, swing to the face on word 1), the phone settling, a gesture, or the live action.
  Never chop the hook line itself; first cut at ≥ 2s unless it's a montage hook.
- **SF6** The hook names a pain, stakes or an event ("will he actually do it?"), not a
  topic or a credential. Claim first; credentials and proof come after the hook.
- **SF7** A top title label from frame 1: either a two-tier persistent title (headline plus
  a smaller parenthetical that opens a loop, e.g. "(the 4th one is why they reply)") or a
  rounded box that disappears at 3–5s.

## Proof and inserts
- **SF8** Proof is real (own screenshots, dashboards, grid, live replies). It pops in
  0.1–0.3s before the number is spoken, holds ~2.8s, top third of the frame, face visible.
- **SF9** Inserts land on the spoken noun, on a hard cut, then get out. No stock clichés.

## Captions (test these against STYLE.md K1)
- **SF10** Plain white captions, no accent-color emphasis (none of the three use one), in
  native-looking sentence case. Run an A/B against K1's violet highlight before adopting.
- **SF11** Caption position: test top-of-frame (above the head) against chest height. Top won
  both of Hayes' same-take tests (2.2–4.6×). Explainers can use one word at a time at chest
  height (Nolan).

## Structure
- **SF12** Two payoffs for event videos: an early stunt or reveal at 25–40% of runtime, then
  the real result (reply, booking, number) at 75–90%.
- **SF13** Open loops in the title parenthetical or an early line ("before I show you the
  results…"); close them before the end.
- **SF14** CTA by format: funnel or results videos get a comment keyword with a real lead
  magnet (needs auto-DM); explainers hold back a sub-list for the keyword or newsletter;
  event, tip and dash videos can go without a CTA.

## Testing discipline
- **SF15** Change one variable per test (hook line, caption position, music, crop). Same take
  otherwise. Log views at 24h and 72h in the video's folder.

## Sound
- **SF16** Loudness −14 to −14.5 LUFS. Music optional; SFX sparse. None of the three lean on
  SFX design. Room, car or road noise is fine for event videos.

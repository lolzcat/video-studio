# Style profile: short-form v1.1 (talking-head and event shorts)

v1 (2026-11-03): 30 reels from @nolan_killeen, @hayeskrause and @cameronprofittt.
v1.1 (2026-11-03): + 36 reels from @vikalp_hirwani (live cold calls), @the_adhd_entrepreneur
(Ari Scott, ADHD business) and @itvignesh.ai (AI agency client proof). See
`~/Obsidian/Luxvoni/Research/References/2026-11-03-creator-study-*.md`.
Structure and production only, never their identity.

**How to use:** tell `/video-script` or `/youtube-edit` "use style short-form-v1". Rules here
override `skills/youtube-edit/STYLE.md` for that video wherever they conflict. Every rule is
*(candidate)* until Andrew confirms it with his own results.

## Pick the format first (edit density follows the format)
- **SF1 Event or story** (live outreach, skit, hot take, results reveal): one continuous take
  or very few cuts (≤ 4/min). No decorative B-roll. The event is the motion.
- **SF2 Explainer or list** ("If I woke up tomorrow…", numbered system): breaths removed
  (~250 wpm), a new angle or header per step, literal inserts or screenshots on the nouns
  covering 40–80% of runtime, a "SCREENSHOT THIS" recap card mid-video.
- **SF3 Dash one-liner**: 12–16s, one take, a 1s pause after the turn word ("but…"), no CTA.
  The best format for one-variable tests.

## Hook (first 3 seconds)
- **SF4** First word spoken in the first frame (≤ 0.3s). No intro, logo or greeting.
- **SF5** Motion in the first second without a cut: a swing-in (start recording pointed
  away, swing to the face on word 1), the phone settling, a gesture, or the live action.
  Never chop the hook line itself; first cut at ≥ 2s unless it's a montage hook.
- **SF6** **The event or the number is in the first second**: the phone picking up, the client
  saying the price, the reply notification, the booked slot. This is the strongest finding of
  both rounds; same-footage tests went 4.1×, 17.8× and 33× for the version that opened on it.
  Otherwise the hook names a pain, stakes or an event ("will he actually do it?"), never a topic
  or a credential. Claim first; credentials come after.
- **SF7** A top title label from frame 1: a two-tier persistent title (headline plus a smaller
  parenthetical that opens a loop, e.g. "(the 4th one is why they reply)"), a "POV: [outcome]"
  line that states the result while the event plays (Vignesh: 3 of his top 4), or a rounded box
  that disappears at 3–5s. For a series, the label is a **running scoreboard**
  (`DAY 7/30 · 42 emails · 3 replies · 1 booked`). A bare day counter didn't add views (Vikalp).

## Proof and inserts
- **SF8** Proof is real (own screenshots, dashboards, grid, live replies). It pops in
  0.1–0.3s before the number is spoken, holds ~2.8s, top third of the frame, face visible.
- **SF9** Inserts land on the spoken noun, on a hard cut, then get out. No stock clichés.
- **SF17** The proof matches the claim exactly: the number said is the number on screen, net not
  gross, one screenshot per client (never reused). (Vignesh's "$1,500" over a ~$810 deposit is
  the cautionary example; it's an FTC income-claim issue too.)
- **SF18** Privacy on real outreach: bleep or blur prospect names, emails, faces and company
  names, and get consent before posting any recorded call (many US states require all-party
  consent). Quote stickers only show what the *other person* actually said.

## Captions (test these against STYLE.md K1)
- **SF10** Plain white captions, no accent-color emphasis (none of the round-1 three use one), in
  native-looking sentence case. Run an A/B against K1's violet highlight before adopting.
  Counterpoint from round 2: Ari's produced reels use one accent word per giant type stack and a
  two-color grammar (mint for wins, pink for failures), and Vignesh colors 1–3-word keywords. So
  color can work when it *means* something; random emphasis doesn't.
- **SF11** Caption position: test top-of-frame (above the head) against chest height. Top won
  both of Hayes' same-take tests (2.2–4.6×). Explainers can use one word at a time at chest
  height (Nolan).

## Event videos (live outreach, client calls)
- **SF19** Live-call skeleton (Vikalp/Cameron): pickup ≤ 1.3s → permission opener (1–5s) → one
  specific observation + yes/no question → the quote/stunt lands at 35–50% → give-first ask →
  the yes + specific time at 75–90% → a fast thank-you and cut within 1.5s. One take, no B-roll.
  For cold email or DMs: the send click → the reply arriving, in split screen, in second one.
- **SF20** Or show, don't tell (Vignesh's hits): no talking to camera, the event plays under a
  "POV:" label, and a beat-cut montage of the payoff (~35–50 cuts/min) on a music bed.

## ADHD content
- **SF21** Name one very specific self-sabotage ("plan a whole launch on a 4-day hyperfocus
  bender, then ghost it"), then blame the system, not the person, with humor inside the
  explanation, then the system that fixes it. "ADHD is a superpower" framing flops (Ari).

## Structure
- **SF12** Two payoffs for event videos: an early stunt or reveal at 25–40% of runtime, then
  the real result (reply, booking, number) at 75–90%.
- **SF13** Open loops in the title parenthetical or an early line ("before I show you the
  results…"); close them before the end.
- **SF14** CTA by format: funnel or results videos get a comment keyword with a real lead
  magnet (needs auto-DM); explainers hold back a sub-list for the keyword or newsletter;
  event, tip and dash videos can go without a CTA.

## Testing discipline
- **SF15** Change one variable per test (hook line, caption position, music, crop). Same take
  otherwise. Log views at 24h and 72h in the video's folder.
- **SF22** Reach is noisy: two near-identical edits of one call came out 7.3× apart (Vikalp). Never
  judge a format on one post. Repeat a test 2–3 times, spaced out, before believing it. Re-edit
  proven footage (tighten it, open on the event) rather than reposting it raw.
- **SF23** Collabs bring positioning, not views (Ari's collabs: a 4K median vs 17K for her own posts).
  Use them for credibility, not reach.

## Sound
- **SF16** Loudness −14 to −14.5 LUFS. Music optional; SFX sparse. None of the three lean on
  SFX design. Room, car or road noise is fine for event videos.
